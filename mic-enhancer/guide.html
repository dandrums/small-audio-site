Mic Enhancer

User guide

From plugging in to a clear, feedback-free voice. The usual order is: connect, ring out, shape, compress, save.

1. Connect & start

  1. Plug the mic into your USB interface, and the interface into your iPhone or iPad. Send the interface's output to the PA or desk.
  2. Open Mic Enhancer and hold the device in landscape.
  3. Tap Start. The status turns green, Running, and shows the sample rate, buffer size and latency.
  4. Speak into the mic and watch the input meter in the top bar. Set the interface's gain so speech stays in the green and only reaches yellow on loud words.

Tap ⓘ for full device details, including input and output names and round-trip latency. Tap ? for tips in the app.

Feedback warning Testing with the built-in mic and speaker will feed back. Use headphones, or a real mic and PA.
Sample rate warning An orange warning means the input and output are running at different sample rates. Audio still works, but it's converted. Set both to the same rate for the lowest latency.

2. Touch controls

  • Tap a dot, band or fader to select it.
  • Press and hold a control for a moment (you'll feel a tap), then drag to change it.
  • A quick swipe never changes a setting. Swipe left or right to move between EQ, Comp and GEQ, or tap them in the top bar.
  • Double-tap a fader to reset it to its default.

3. Ring out with the graphic EQ

Mic Enhancer opens on GEQ, because ringing out is the first job in a new room. Do it with the mic where the speaker will stand.

  1. Make sure GEQ and RTA (the analyser) are on. The blue spectrum shows what's going to the outputs. Turn on Peak hold for an orange line of recent peaks, and tap Clear to reset it.
  2. Bring the PA up slowly until it just starts to ring.
  3. Feedback shows as a narrow spike. The ▼ marks the band nearest the loudest frequency, and the Loudest readout shows its frequency and level. Tap Go to to select that band.
  4. Pull that band below 0 dB until the ringing stops. Use ◀ ▶ and the side fader for fine control.
  5. Bring the PA up a little more and repeat for the next frequency that rings. Two to four cuts is typical.

Some engineers push a band up on purpose to find the frequency that rings, then cut it. Boosted bands turn orange, so you can see any left in. Reset sets every band back to 0 dB.

Tip Small, narrow cuts at the problem frequencies are better than a big cut across many bands, which makes a voice thin.

4. Channel EQ

Shape the voice on the EQ screen.

High-pass filter

Turn on HPF to remove rumble, stage noise and mic handling. Its slope is 12 dB/oct. Set Freq as high as you can before the voice sounds thin, typically 80–150 Hz.

Parametric bands

  1. Pick a band (Low, Mid, High) with the band button, or tap its dot on the graph.
  2. Drag the dot left or right for frequency, and up or down to boost or cut.
  3. Pinch on the graph to change the band's Q (width).
  4. Use the Freq, Gain and Q faders on the right for exact values.

To find a frequency that sounds bad: boost a narrow band, sweep it until the problem stands out, then pull it below 0 dB. Reset boosts clears any boost left in and keeps your cuts.

EQ switches the whole channel EQ on and off, for a quick before/after comparison. Band switches the selected band on and off. The faint background on the graph is the live output spectrum.

5. Compressor

The Comp screen evens out level, so a speaker who leans in or turns away stays clear. It works in three bands (low, mid, high), so a boomy word doesn't pull down the whole voice.

  1. Turn on Active.
  2. Each band is a "hill" on the graph that drops as the band compresses. Drag a band's white arrow down to lower its threshold until the hill moves on louder words.
  3. Drag the white handles at the top to move the crossovers between bands.
  4. Drag a band's hill up to add makeup gain, up to +6 dB.
  5. Set Ratio, Attack and Release for the selected band with the faders on the right.

Solo lets you hear only the selected band, which helps when you set crossovers. Solo is always off when a preset loads. Band turns the selected band on and off.

Starting point for speech Ratio 3:1 and a threshold that gives a few dB of reduction on normal speech. Leave attack and release at their defaults.

6. Presets

Tap the presets button in the top bar.

  • Save as new preset… saves the EQ, compressor and graphic EQ settings. Name it after the venue or the speaker.
  • Tap a preset to load it. Swipe left on a preset to Rename or Delete it.
  • If you change a loaded preset, it's marked Modified since saved. Tap Update to save the changes into it.
  • Reset all settings to defaults sets everything flat. Saved presets are kept.

You don't have to save a preset for your settings to be kept. Mic Enhancer remembers the current settings when it closes. Device settings such as sample rate aren't part of a preset, because they belong to the hardware.

7. Show Mode

A text or call arriving mid-talk can interrupt the sound. Show mode runs two Shortcuts you create, so you can silence your device in one tap before you start.

  1. Open the Shortcuts app and create a shortcut named exactly Mic Enhancer Show Mode ON. Add actions such as Set Focus (Do Not Disturb), Set Brightness or Set Auto-Lock.
  2. Create a second shortcut named Mic Enhancer Show Mode OFF that undoes them.
  3. In Mic Enhancer, tap Show mode. Shortcuts runs and returns you to the app. The button turns from red to green.
Tip Turn Show Mode on before you tap Start for the talk, and off again afterwards.

Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
Status shows StoppedTap Start. If a red error line appears, check that the interface is connected and powered, then try again.
No sound outCheck ⓘ to see which output device Mic Enhancer is using. Check the interface's output level and the PA input.
Meter not movingCheck the interface's input gain and phantom power for condenser mics.
Voice sounds thinLower the HPF frequency, and check the GEQ for too many deep cuts.
Voice sounds squashedRaise the compressor thresholds or lower the ratio.
Analyser is blankTurn on RTA. The analyser only runs while audio is running.
